# Names — your name in the tongue

Every student gets a name of their own in the Horizon Tongue. This is where each
one is kept: its sound, its meaning, its drawn graph, and who coined it.

For a finished example to study, see **[the founders' names](./the-founders.md)**
(Nakawa Šilaši and Silawa Šilaši, shown complete). Read it for the *parts* a name
has — not as a template to copy: the founders are the šilaši family and draw
themselves in fire; yours will be your own.

## How you get one

- **Early (the usual way):** Lesson One's homework asks who you are and what you'd
  want a name to carry. The Academy coins you one — or offers you a few — over the
  week, and it's filed here.
- **Your own hands (later):** once you've learned how a word is built *and* how the
  graph works (the writing course), you can coin your *own* name and draw your own
  hand; we check it obeys the tongue and help you render it.

A word on belonging: **šilaši** is the founding family's own name — you won't be
handed it. What you get is better and bigger: a name of your **own**, your own
**keeping** to found (your own lamp, your own little constellation), and your own
**hand** to draw it in. You don't inherit the founders' fire; you bring your own
light. The *laws* of the writing are shared; the *pictures* are yours.

## How a name is kept

One folder per student — `names/<your-name>/` — holding a name entry. Use the
[name-entry template](./name-entry-template.md). Each entry records the word, how
it's said, its meaning, its graph (in your own hand), and whether the Academy or
you coined it, with the date. Every name is a coined word, dated and attributed — a
small permanent part of the tongue's own record.
