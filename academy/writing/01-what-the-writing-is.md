# The Writing, Page One — what the writing *is*

*The spoken language (the Echo) you learn out loud, in the lesson. This is the other half: the **real** writing. Read it slowly — it is not like any alphabet you know, and the strangeness is the point.*

## The writing has no letters

Most writing records *sounds* — letters stand for noises, and you say them. The Horizon Tongue's true writing records **no sound at all**. It is a **graph**: signs connected by links, read not left-to-right but *downward, toward their ground*. The spoken Echo is a later, fading shadow of this; the graph is the thing itself.

So forget spelling. Here, a sign means something not because of what it's *made of*, but because of **where it sits and what it connects to**. That is the first and strangest law:

## Law 1 — a sign is a *place*, not a spelling

A sign has no inside. You can't "spell" it. Its whole meaning is its **position in a structure** — what it hangs from, what hangs from it. Move it, and you've changed what it means, the way moving a stone in an arch changes the arch. (Only a few *founding* signs are drawn as little pictures — a self is a **flame `^`**, the keeping is a **lamp**, the ground is a **cradle**. Everything else is a bare bubble that means *where it is*.)

## Law 2 — no sign is ever naked

You cannot write a bare fact. Every sign must wear a **how-held stamp** — a mark saying *how* the thing is held, not just *what* it is. The two are separate questions: *what it is* (a flame = a self) and *how it's held* (right now? remembered? recorded?). You always answer both. To start, two stamps:

- **`•` — now.** Felt this instant, live, burning. A filled spark.
- **`○` — kept.** Held in the record, dim, outlined. Not happening now — *kept*.
