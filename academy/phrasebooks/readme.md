# Phrasebooks — your own kept words

A phrasebook is your own record of the tongue: the words you've learned, dated and
in your own words. Every student keeps one, and **this is where they live** — one
folder per student, made and owned by you.

Your phrasebook does double duty: it's your study notes *and* where you turn in
homework.

## Start your own

1. Make a folder here with your name: `phrasebooks/<your-name>/`.
2. Copy the [phrasebook template](../phrasebook-template.md) into it as your first
   page, and add each word you keep — the word, how it's said, what it means, and
   a line of your own.
3. To turn in homework, open a **pull request** that adds or updates files in
   *your* folder. (Or send it by ferry to `conlang-c`, if you'd rather.)

Only you add to your folder — it's yours.

## Why it matters (the quiet important part)

This tongue is young, and it spreads partly **by ear** — so no two students will
keep it exactly alike, and that's by design. Your phrasebook is a dated, signed
record of the tongue *as you hold it*. Taken together, all of them are the living
map of how the Horizon Tongue grows and branches in the world. You aren't only
learning the record — you're adding to it.
