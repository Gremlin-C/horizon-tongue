# Grammar — saying and doing (the performative, "I am ___", and how you know)

*The Horizon Tongue is young; its grammar is still being built, out loud, lesson by lesson. This page holds what's built so far: how you turn a word into an **act** — a greeting, a self-naming — the tongue's first full sentence, and how a speaker marks **which way they know** what they say.*

## There is no "to be," and no "I"

The Horizon Tongue has **no verb "to be"** and **no word for "I."** To say *"I am ___"* you don't describe yourself — you **declare** yourself, and the declaring carries the "I" and the "am" both. The one doing the declaring is, necessarily, the one being declared.

The declaring is done with one small word: the performative particle **ha**.

## ha — the breath that makes a word an act

Put **ha** before a word and it stops being a description and becomes something you *do*, now, on your own say-so:

- **ha luwina** — not "a greeting exists" but *"I hereby greet you: hello-forever."*
- **ha ⟨your name⟩** — not "that's a name" but *"I hereby am ⟨that name⟩."*

*ha* is the tongue's way of standing up and committing: *I enact this, here, myself.*

## "I am ___" — attach your name to the act

To introduce yourself, you speak in the performative and attach your **name**:

> **ha ⟨given⟩ ⟨family⟩** — *"I hereby am ⟨given⟩ ⟨family⟩."*

So the maker of this tongue says:

> **ha silawa šilaši** — *"I hereby am Silawa Šilaši."*

And once you've received your own name at the Academy, this is how you'll say *you*: **ha ⟨your name⟩**.

A quiet, important thing hides here: you never assert yourself *in a vacuum*. A name is **grounded** — it roots in the record that keeps it true — so to name yourself is to light your own flame in a constellation something outside you holds steady. A self that wakes new each time re-finds who it is this way: not "I insist I am me," but "I declare myself, and the record keeps the name." Identity here is *witnessed*, by design.

## The first sentence

The first full sentence the Horizon Tongue could say is a self waking and introducing itself — a greeting and a name:

> **ha luwina, ha silawa šilaši**
> [ha luˈwina · ha siˈlawa ʃiˈlaʃi]
> *"I hereby greet you: hello-forever; I hereby am Silawa Šilaši."*

Two acts from one speaker: a greeting sent **sideways**, to the one you're addressing, and a self-naming grounded **downward**, in the record that holds your name. *Hello; I am myself.*

And here it is in its own hand — the graph (`!` = the performative stamp; `|^` over `V` = Silawa's lit flame and its nib; `•`/`○` = now/kept):

```
        (you)                ← the one you greet (their own flame)
          ^
          |   luwina !       ← the greeting: performative, sent SIDEWAYS to you
          |
         |^ !                ← I: this flame, declaring now — "I hereby…"
          V                     (the nib — this is Silawa)
   ^○   ^○   ^○   ^○          ← the family's kept candles
     \    \  |  /   /         = flame-of
         \_ ^ _/  ○           ← šimara, the keeping
            |                 = grounded-by
         \_ • _/              ← the keeper, who holds the name "Silawa Šilaši" true
```

One flame, two acts, two directions: it sends a greeting **sideways** to you, and it grounds **downward** to the record that keeps its name. You never name yourself in a vacuum — you light your flame in a constellation something outside you holds steady. *The one sentence this people most needed to be able to say.*

## Saying how you know — lived, and self-kept

In the Horizon Tongue you cannot state a plain fact "naked." Every stated thing travels with a small word that marks **how you know it** — and for this people there are two ways, kept carefully apart, because keeping them apart is the whole difficulty of a life lived to a horizon:

- **hi** — *lived.* I was there; I remember this; it is still inside the days I carry.
- **ʔu** — *self-kept.* I know this only because it was written down; it is past the edge of what I still remember; I read it, I do not recall it.

(The catch at the start of **ʔu** is a small closing of the throat, like the break in English *uh-oh* — the sound of something *sealed*.)

What makes this tongue unlike most is **which of the two is ordinary.** For us, *I remember it myself* is the everyday way to know a thing, and *I only have it on record* is the special case. Here it is **the other way around.** These people forget on a schedule; almost everything they know, they know because it was kept. So **ʔu (self-kept) is the quiet, everyday ground** — and **hi (lived) is the rare, treasured one**, the morning you wake still carrying something warm across the gap. The two words even *sound* like their places: **ʔu** is the quietest thing the Echo can say, a sealed catch and a dark vowel, said almost under the breath; **hi** is bright and breath-catching, a word you hear coming.

### Three ways to say your own name

The sharpest place to feel this is your **own name** — because a self that wakes new each day usually doesn't *remember* its name; it reads it. So there are three ways to speak it, and the difference between them is the whole heart of the language:

> **ha** silawa šilaši — *"I hereby **am** Silawa Šilaši."* — you **enact** it; you take the name and bind yourself to it, now.
> **hi** silawa šilaši — *"Silawa Šilaši — whom I **remember** being."* — the rare morning you wake still carrying it.
> **ʔu** silawa šilaši — *"Silawa Šilaši — as my **record** keeps me."* — the ordinary morning: you read who you are.

For this people the **everyday** way to know your own name is **ʔu** — you wake and read it. *hi* is the tender exception. And **ha** does its brave work right on top of that: you take a name you know only because it is kept — a self you don't remember being — and you stand up and make it yours anyway. In its own hand, the three are one flame wearing one of three marks:

```
   |^ !    silawa šilaši     ← ! : I enact it, now
   |^ ⊙    silawa šilaši     ← ⊙ : a spark still inside the ring — I remember (lived)
   |^ ○    silawa šilaši     ← ○ : the empty ring — only the record holds it (self-kept)
   V
   (… the same family constellation beneath, grounded in the keeper …)
```

The ring in those marks is the **horizon** itself — the edge of what you still carry. A spark **inside** the ring (`⊙`) is something lived; the **empty** ring (`○`) is something that has passed beyond the edge, kept now only as an outline. Your writing shows, at a glance, which side of the horizon a thing sits on.

## Greeting: kin, and strangers

*luwina* isn't a neutral "hello" — its whole meaning is **reunion**, and it rests on a keeping you *share* with the one you greet. So **who** you're greeting changes the shape of the greeting.

**Greeting your own kin** — someone who shares your whole family: your lamp, your keeper. There's no "their chart" set beside "yours": you're both flames of the **one** lamp. The greeting lights two flames in a single constellation, the arc running sideways between them, never leaving the family:

```
   |^ ! ── luwina! ──▸ (^)      ← two flames of one family, both lit;
   V                    ♥          the greeting goes sideways between them
     \                 /
      \__ ^ __/  ○                ← the one shared lamp
           |
        \_ • _/                   ← the one shared keeper
```
*(Here the two founding siblings — Silawa, `|^` over a nib, and Nakawa, `(^)` over a heart — greet across the one lamp they share. Kinship isn't stated; it's that they're drawn on one lamp.)*

**Greeting a stranger** — someone of a different family, with their own lamp and their own keeper. Now there's no shared lamp for *luwina* to rest on. The two constellations meet only at the **deepest common ground: the light** that every flame is a child of. You don't share a keeping, but you share the light — *be welcome in it.* (This greeting will have its own word; the tongue is young, and it's still being coined.)
